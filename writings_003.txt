The Shepherd -
I awoke, sitting in the corridor, of a hospital, with double doors, locked at each side. 
An exit sign above each pair.
Looking down, I was wearing the attire of a patient.
A gown. 
White, cotton, with checkered blue diamonds stitched all across. 
There were two women. 
Same gowns.
Pale. 
Sick.
I could tell.
Seeing my own skin.
I was pale. 
Sick.
It was bright.
The lights drowned the color surrounding them in white.
The women were scared. 
Words unneeded.
Something was coming.
Yahweh called to me. 
You haven't the time. 
Run.
I took one of each of their hands in each of mine.
And ran.
Both of the women struck the doors in unison with me.
The lock broke.
But we were dumped into the same corridor. 
One light was now flickering. 
Water began to flood from each side of the corridor.
Covering the ground with a thin layer of consistently encroaching water. 
The water was clear.
But I knew it was unclean. 
This water had made us sick.
So we ran. 
Through the water.
Both of the women struck the doors in unison with me.
The lock broke.
But we were dumped into the same corridor. 
One light was now flickering. 
The ceiling was beginning to cave. 
And water was leaking.
The flood was the same as before.
But deeper.
And faster. 
So we ran faster. 
Through the water.
Both of the women struck the doors in unison with me.
The lock broke.
But we were dumped into the same corridor. 
The corridor was collapsing. 
The water was at the height of the top of my head.
The flood was rushing. 
So we ran back. 
The broken had been repaired.
The unlocked locked. 
Both of the women struck the doors in unison with me.
The lock broke.
I felt the warmth of sunshine on my face.
We were standing at the bottom of a stone staircase.
It reached up toward the surface.
From where the sunlight was coming.
I was wet.
They were wet.
But the corridor was gone.
And there was a wall of dirt there instead.
So we walked up the steps.
And as my head rose above the ground, I could see that we were standing at the summit of a medium, dome shaped hill with very short deep, green grass, spreading endlessly in each direction.  
The sun was setting, behind a large mountain.
Covered by the green grass. 
My head, as well as the women's, stuck up from the staircase, observing.
There was a few miles of green expanse. 
Between the dome and the mountain.
The light from the sun was beginning to dim as it tucked behind the mountain.
The drowning white diminished. 
And I could see.
Beneath the mountain.
Was a group. 
Of livestock.
They were giant.
Like bison.
With the wool of sheep.
But amber red. 
And the weight of the wool, locking under gravity, growing long, but never reaching the ground.
At the front. 
Was a Shepherd.
With white hair.
And a long, white beard.
Not messy, or unkempt.
Perfectly kept.
Their movement was slow.
And with the Sun. 
In another time.
When the livestock had passed the threshold of half of the mountain, the Shepherd extended his staff and planted it out in front of himself at a perfectly slanted angle, shifting his head out of synchronization with the Sun, faster than he or the livestock had moved before, he fixed his gaze directly on mine. 
At that moment, the Sun glistened, in his left eye, and twinkled into both of mine.
His mouth moved slightly.
Not a smirk.
Not a smile.
But an acknowledgment.
I averted my eyes immediately.
It was too much.
Overwhelming.
And I was overcome with agony.
It was devastation.
A reckoning.
A shattering.
Bedrock.
Breaking.
I knew that the Shepherd knew.
That I would not know.
What the Shepherd had done.
